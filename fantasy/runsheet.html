01 The one rule

Take the player with the highest wait cost — not the highest value, not the biggest name.

Wait cost is a player's value minus what you'd expect to still get at that position at your next pick. A player nobody else wants for ten picks is free; you can have him later. The tool computes it live from a simulation of the picks in between, and it is the only number on the table you have to read.

This was measured, not assumed: +30 ± 2 points a season over drafting straight down the consensus list, across five replayed seasons, winning in all five. It is the single largest edge in the toolkit.

03 During the draft — the loop

There are only two things you ever do, and one of them is automatic.

EVERY PICK

Type the name of whoever just went

jeanty                # someone else took him
me nacua              # you took him ("+nacua" also works)

Fuzzy matching, so ja'marr and bijan both land. Your keeper fills its own slot at pick 6 — don't type it. If a pick is yours and you forget me, it records it as yours anyway and says so.

YOUR TURN

The table appears on its own

You don't need to type anything — when the pick before yours is entered, the recommendation prints automatically. go reprints it any time.

Reading the columns

ColumnWhat it isHow to use it
WAIT COST Value now minus expected best available at that position at your next pick. This is the pick. Highest number wins.
SURVIVE Chance he's still there at your next pick, from a live simulation of the picks in between. Context for the number above. Under ~20% means now or never.
TIER Set by how much the experts disagree — while rank ranges overlap, the panel can't separate them and neither should you. Inside a tier, take the scarcer position or the better playoff schedule.
VORP Points above the last startable player at that position, flex-aware. Background only. Don't draft off it.
LY 2025 points over expected per game. yellow outran his usage, green underran it. Tiebreaker between close players. Fade yellow harder than you chase green — see §06.

06 Decisions already made

Each of these was measured against real seasons. Under a clock, don't relitigate them.

QuestionAnswerEvidence
Draft by wait cost, or by best available? Wait cost. +30 ± 2 / season, 5/5 seasons
Should a big snap share or target share move a player up? No. Tiebreaker only — the consensus already priced it. −12 to −53, lost every way tested
Chase last year's touchdown scorers? Fade them. Hot players gave back 91% of the gap; cold ones recovered only 31%. 4 seasons, fade ≈3× more reliable
Zero-RB or RB-heavy from pick 6? Neither. Both swing wildly by season; wait cost was the only rule positive in all five. Zero-RB +104 → −118 by season
Kicker and defense? Last two rounds. Never earlier. zero VORP by construction
Backup QB or TE on the bench? Fine. Discounting them was tried and lost. reverted after measurement

07 When something goes wrong

SymptomDo this
A name matches several players and it asks which? Type the number, or type more of the name. A command word (go, undo) cancels out cleanly.
You typed the wrong player undo, then retype. Repeat undo to walk back further.
Yahoo's pick number and the terminal's disagree You dropped or double-entered a pick. undo back to where they agree and re-enter. Glance at this every few picks — the prompt shows [pick N].
Terminal closed or laptop slept Re-run python3 draft_day.py. It resumes from draft_state.json and tells you how many picks it restored.
It says a player is already off the board He was already entered, or he's a keeper. Check with board.
Everything is broken and the clock is running Printed cheat sheet. Take the best player at a position you still need, preferring the one with a longer wait until your next pick.

The one unrecoverable mistake

A pick entered wrong and left wrong. Every pick number after it is then off by one, and every survival estimate is computed against the wrong clock for the rest of the draft. Nothing else on this page matters as much as keeping the count honest.

08 After the draft

  • Save the state file. draft_state.json is the record of who took what — it's the seed for opponent modelling next year.
  • Waiver priority is a depleting resource. Rolling list, not FAAB, so every claim costs you position. Spend it on workload changes, not on one big week.
  • Watch opportunity, not points. Measured at +28 ± 4 a season versus +17 for chasing the scoreboard — the difference between them, +11 ± 3, is real. This is where usage data does pay, because in October the market is stale in a way it never is in August.
  • Your playoffs are weeks 15–17, six of eight teams make it, and benched players aren't locked — so late-swap is available every week.
